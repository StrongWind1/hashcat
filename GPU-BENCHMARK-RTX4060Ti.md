# WPA-PSK Universal Modes (90001–90010) — GPU Speed Document

Throughput record for the universal WPA-PSK bake-off on a real GPU, using fresh, equal-dataset corpora generated by `WPAWolf/tools/corpusgen/wpa_corpusgen.py` (no data reused between runs).

**Methodology note — read this first.** An initial 398-run sweep used hashcat `--speed-only` (its autotuned estimate). A sustained-run confirmation then showed `--speed-only` is **not reliable** for this comparison: it agrees with real runs in the simplest case (single-salt CUDA) but *over*-states OpenCL single-hash by ~30% and *under*-states dense CUDA by ~2×, and it inflated an apparent "OpenCL ≈ 2× CUDA" that does not exist. **All numbers below are real, sustained runs** (`--runtime`, a stabilized `Speed.#1` over a 10M-candidate list), which is the ground truth. Even sustained *absolute* throughput is noisy across different salt/digest structures, so every comparison here is made **within a single corpus** (universal-vs-22000 and CUDA-vs-OpenCL on the identical hash list), where the ratio is trustworthy; cross-density absolute kH/s is not presented as a scaling curve. Raw data: `GPU-BENCHMARK-RTX4060Ti.sustained.tsv`.

## Test bed

- GPU: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Ti, 16 GB (rented, Vast.ai); driver 595.71.05, CUDA 13.2, OpenCL 3.0. CUDA and OpenCL are the only two backends this hardware exposes.
- hashcat `v7.1.2` from fork `feat/wpa-psk-universal-modes`, after the `REAL_SHM` AES T-table fix.
- Corpora: deterministic, self-test/KAT validated, every hash independently cracked 100% in hashcat. Apples-to-apples corpora emit the same handshakes in both the universal and the stock `-m 22000` format.

## Headline: universal vs stock 22000 on identical handshakes (sustained)

| Surface | Density (salts × digests) | CUDA universal ÷ 22000 | OpenCL universal ÷ 22000 |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|
| EAPOL (type 03 / stock 02) | 1 × 1 | **2.37×** | 1.00× |
| EAPOL | 10 × 100 | 1.36× | 1.00× |
| EAPOL | 100 × 10000 | 1.13× | 1.00× |
| PMKID (type 02 / stock 01) | 1 × 1 | **2.20×** | 1.01× |
| PMKID | 100 × 10000 | 1.11× | 1.04× |

- **On CUDA the universal modes are faster than stock 22000 at every density** — dramatically so at a single hash (~2.2–2.4×), narrowing to ~1.1× on a dense list. The gap is a CUDA-codegen difference: NVRTC produces a much slower kernel for stock 22000 than for the universal aux split, and 22000 closes most of the gap only when a dense salt structure lets it amortize that overhead.
- **On OpenCL the two are tied** (within ~1–4%) at every density.
- **22000 is never favored.** The earlier "22000 dominates the dense OpenCL workload" claim was an unequal digest count; the earlier "universal is ~2× faster on CUDA across all densities" overstated a single-hash effect that shrinks to ~1.1× when dense. The honest statement: on equal handshakes, universal beats 22000 on CUDA (by a density-dependent margin) and ties it on OpenCL.

## Backend: OpenCL ≈ CUDA (not 2×)

Best universal mode on the identical corpus, OpenCL ÷ CUDA: **1.13× (1×1), 1.04× (10×100), 1.07× (100×10000) for EAPOL; 1.14× / 0.99× for PMKID.** OpenCL is at most ~14% faster than CUDA and is essentially tied on dense lists — there is no 2× backend advantage. (The `--speed-only` estimate that suggested one was the artifact described above.)

## Per-backend ranking (mean finish across the five corpora)

Mean finish, fastest → slowest (this five-corpus set is WPA2/PMKID-weighted; the mixed-traffic order differs — see the bullets below):

| Backend | mean-rank order |
|---|---|
| **CUDA** | 90001 (1.8) · 90002 (2.4) · 90006 (3.6) · 90009 (4.6) · 90008 (5.2) · 90005 (5.4) · 90004 (6.0) · 90010 (7.4) · 90007 (8.6) · 90003 (10.0) |
| **OpenCL** | 90009 (2.6) · 90002 (2.8) · 90005 (3.0) · 90001 (3.8) · 90004 (5.2) · 90006 (5.2) · 90008 (5.4) · 90010 (8.4) · 90007 (8.6) · 90003 (10.0) |

- **90001 (monolithic-comp) has a real edge on CUDA** — looping all digests in one comp kernel avoids per-digest aux launches, which dominate under NVRTC; it falls to fourth on OpenCL where its register-heavy monolith costs occupancy.
- **The remaining aux designs differ by real margins, not noise — and the order depends on the workload.** A 5-repeat measurement puts run-to-run noise at ~0.2–2% CV (90001 dense CUDA = 305.9 ± 0.5 kH/s), so the ~10–15% spreads between designs are genuine signal. Which design leads depends on the list: on WPA2-only-dense 90001 leads CUDA; on a realistic all-types **mixed** list the CUDA top is a cluster — 90009 (147) ≈ 90004 (144) ≈ 90001 (137) — and OpenCL is led by 90005 (179) › 90002 (169) › 90009 (167). 90002 (4-family) is the most consistent across backends; 90004 is mid on WPA2-only but in the top cluster on mixed.
- **90003 (all-CPU validate), 90007 (hybrid) and 90010 (branchless) are the floor on both backends** — host round-trips and eleven-fold redundant verify, unaffected by the backend.

## PMKID vs EAPOL surface

The two attack surfaces behave the same: best-universal at 1×1 is CUDA 278 / OpenCL 315 kH/s for EAPOL and CUDA 278 / OpenCL 317 for PMKID; both give CUDA ~2.2–2.4× over 22000 and OpenCL tied. The surface split inside 90004 does not make PMKID and EAPOL diverge in this comparison.

## Per-AKM cost and the non-22000 types (sustained)

All eight types `-m 22000` cannot represent build, crack and benchmark on both backends and all ten modes. Per-AKM cost (mode 90004, dense, sustained kH/s) tracks the crypto and is the reason these modes exist:

| Type | CUDA | OpenCL | | Type | CUDA | OpenCL |
|---|--:|--:|---|---|--:|--:|
| SHA256 PMKID (04) | 224.9 | 291.5 | | SHA384 PMKID (08) | 233.6 | 284.7 |
| SHA256 EAPOL-CMAC (05) | 89.9 | 104.6 | | SHA384 EAPOL (09) | 47.1 | 50.4 |
| FT256 PMKID (06) | 213.6 | 293.3 | | FT384 PMKID (10) | 212.7 | 269.7 |
| FT256 EAPOL-CMAC (07) | 194.0 | 232.0 | | FT384 EAPOL (11) | 133.4 | 180.5 |

PMKID types are cheap (one HMAC, ~210–235 CUDA); EAPOL types pay for the PTK plus the MIC over the frame; **SHA-384 EAPOL (09) is the heaviest by far (~47 kH/s)** — 64-bit words and a 24-byte MIC — with the AES-CMAC types (05/07) next. On the SHA-384 EAPOL type the host-hook designs collapse (90003/90007 → ~6 kH/s — 90007 offloads exactly the SHA-384 slice, 90003 offloads everything) and branchless 90010 drops to ~19, while the GPU-native designs stay clustered ~47–50, confirming the hook penalty lands precisely where the design puts it.

## What this means for shipping 22002

- **All ten universal modes beat (CUDA) or tie (OpenCL) stock 22000 on equal data** — shipping any of them is a net win, and the eleven-type loader carries no speed penalty.
- **The design differences are real (noise ~1–2%), modest (~10–20%), and workload-dependent.** No design wins everywhere: 90001 leads WPA2-dense on CUDA; the mixed-traffic top is a 90009/90004/90002 cluster on CUDA and 90005/90002 on OpenCL; the host-hook (90003, 90007) and branchless (90010) designs are clearly slowest everywhere and collapse on SHA-384.
- **For a general-purpose production mode the representative workload is mixed all-types, where 90004 sits in the top cluster (CUDA second) while staying core-clean and the structurally simplest fast design — so it remains a defensible default for `22002`.** If a single data-driven pick is preferred, **90002 (4-family) or 90009 (3-tier)** are marginally more consistent across backends and also core-clean; **90001** is the WPA2-dense CUDA peak (register-heavy monolith); **90005** leads OpenCL but needs the core patch. All ten plugins are kept in-tree as the bake-off record.

## Concepts & lessons

Distilled takeaways — the parts most useful to a hashcat developer.

### Backend & compiler
- **CUDA (NVRTC) and OpenCL (NVIDIA) compile the same `.cl` source very differently.** Starkest case: stock `-m 22000`'s comp kernel is ~2× slower under NVRTC than under NVIDIA's OpenCL compiler, while the universal aux-split is not — same hardware, same algorithm, the compiler is the variable. Treat the two backends as two code generators, not one device with two front-ends.
- **OpenCL is ~tied with CUDA here (≤1.14×)**, not structurally faster. Any large backend gap reported by `--speed-only` is an autotune artifact.
- **NVIDIA OpenCL needs the ICD registration** (`/etc/OpenCL/vendors/nvidia.icd`); the driver lib and loader can be present while the platform is invisible (`CL_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND_KHR`).
- **`REAL_SHM` (local memory) is defined on both GPU backends** — which is why the AES T-table address-space bug only ever surfaced on a GPU, never on the PoCL CPU.

### Workload shape (what actually moves the numbers)
- **ESSID = the PBKDF2 salt; the PMK is derived once per ESSID.** Many ESSIDs → the 4096-round PBKDF2 dominates and every architecture looks identical; one ESSID with many handshakes → PBKDF2 amortizes and the per-digest **verify dispatch** becomes the differentiator. That amortization is the entire reason this format exists.
- **PMKID is cheaper than EAPOL** (one keyed hash vs derive-PTK-then-MIC-over-frame), but the two behave identically in the universal-vs-22000 and backend comparisons — the surface is a cost difference, not a behavior difference.
- **Mixed-type lists reorder the winners** vs single-type lists; real traffic is mixed, so the mixed ranking is the production-relevant one.
- **Absolute throughput is not comparable across different salt/digest structures** (noise + occupancy effects) — only within-structure ratios are trustworthy.

### The eleven types
- **All eleven share one 256-bit PMK** = PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA1 (a passphrase cannot yield a 384-bit PMK). KCK is 16 B for the SHA-1/256 families, 24 B for SHA-384; the MIC is 16 B except SHA-384 EAPOL (09/11) = 24 B (192-bit).
- **Cost tracks the crypto:** PMKID cheap; EAPOL pricier; **SHA-384 EAPOL (09) heaviest** (64-bit words, 24-B MIC); AES-CMAC (05/07) costly. FT types (06/07/10/11) add the PMK-R0→R1 hierarchy and use `PMKR1Name` (a truncated nested hash, not a plain HMAC); FT-384 name hashes must use SHA-384 — a common foot-gun (fixturegen got it wrong; hashcat's verifier is the oracle).
- **The host-hook designs (90003/90007) collapse precisely on the SHA-384 EAPOL types** they offload, confirming the offload cost lands where the design puts it.

### Design / GPU architecture
- **The "eliminate SIMT divergence" hypothesis did not hold.** The branchless superset (90010, all eleven verifiers per digest) is near the bottom everywhere — redundant crypto outweighs divergence savings — and the per-type design (90005) wins only on OpenCL, a compiler quirk, not a divergence law. On a PBKDF2-dominated, per-digest-dispatch workload, "do less work" and "issue fewer launches" beat "remove branches."
- **Per-digest kernel-launch overhead is the dominant verify-side cost on CUDA** — the single in-kernel digest loop (90001) wins CUDA by issuing one launch where the aux designs issue one per digest.

### Methodology (transferable beyond WPA)
- **`--speed-only` is an autotuned estimate, not a measurement** — it matched reality only for single-salt CUDA and was off 30%–2× elsewhere. Use real `--runtime` runs for any cross-backend or cross-design claim.
- **Measure the noise floor before calling anything a tie.** Here it is ~0.2–2% CV, which made the ~10–15% design spreads real signal, not noise.
- **Equal datasets are non-negotiable** — the original "22000 is favored" was purely a 68-vs-155 digest confound; the deterministic generator (`WPAWolf/tools/corpusgen`) exists to kill that error class.

## Optimization opportunities (for the maintainer)

The PBKDF2/SIMD/AES core is **already byte-identical to stock `m22000`** — the ipad/opad-once PBKDF2 inner loop, the `VECT_SIZE` SIMD `_loop`, the AES T-table + `REAL_SHM` local copy, and the CMAC `make_kn` subkeys are all verbatim, so there is no speedup to find there. The real levers are in dispatch and per-salt amortization, which matter only because of this format's many-handshakes-per-ESSID workload that stock never targets:

1. **Adopt the monolithic comp-loop (`OPTS_TYPE_MULTIHASH_DESPITE_ESALT`, as in 90001) over per-digest aux launches** — the highest-impact change. The deep-comp dispatch issues one kernel launch *per digest* per salt; the comp-loop issues one and iterates digests in-kernel. This is exactly why 90001 wins CUDA at high digest counts. Tradeoff: the monolith carries every primitive (MD5/SHA-1/256/384/AES) and switches eleven ways, raising register pressure and hurting occupancy on the heavy paths — so ship it behind a measured A/B, and consider `noinline` on the FT/SHA-384 verifiers to keep the common SHA-1/256 path's live-register set small. Helps many-per-ESSID and mixed on CUDA; neutral/negative at one-digest-per-salt or on OpenCL.
2. **Hoist the PMK HMAC ipad/opad midstate once per PMK and reuse it across PMKID digests** — algorithmically on-thesis (saves two hash-block transforms per extra PMKID sharing a PMK, largest for SHA-384), but honest impact is sub-1% on a normal crack because the verifier is dwarfed by 4096-round PBKDF2. Worth doing for correctness-of-design, not as a perf headline.

In short: the core is optimal; the wins are structural (launch count) and only material on the dense, many-per-ESSID workload — present #1 as a CUDA-specific design A/B, not a universal speedup.
